Editing your website, made simple.

A short guide to your new editing system. You can now change the words on your website yourself, directly in the browser, with no files, no uploads, and nothing you can break.

Step One · The Big Picture

How your new system works.

Your website now has a professional editing surface called CloudCannon. You open your website inside it, click on a piece of text, type your change, and press Save. A few minutes later the change is live on executiveframe.co.nz. That is the whole process.

Click the words, then type.

Editable text shows a thin outline when you move your mouse over it. Click once and a cursor appears, along with a small formatting toolbar. Edit the words exactly as you would in a Word document. Anything without an outline is locked and simply will not respond to clicks.

Step Four · Going Live

Save once, and it publishes itself.

When you have finished editing, press the blue Save button at the top right. That is your only job; everything after that is automatic.

What happens after you press save

1. Your change is recorded in the website's master copy, with a full history, so nothing is ever lost and anything can be undone by Charlotte.

2. The system rebuilds the website with your new wording.

3. Within a few minutes, the change is live at executiveframe.co.nz. There is nothing to upload and no one to wait for.

If you start editing and change your mind, use the menu (⋮, top right) and choose Discard unsaved changes. The page returns to exactly how it was, and nothing is published.

Step Five · Boundaries

Yours to edit, and locked.

Across the nineteen content pages, 652 pieces of text are open to you. Everything else is locked on purpose, which is what makes the system safe to use freely.

You can edit
  • Page titles and section headings
  • The small label lines above headings
  • Body paragraphs
  • Card headings, tags and descriptions
  • Button wording (e.g. “The Science”)
Locked
  • All nine assessment pages
  • Navigation menu and footer
  • Diagrams, images and animations
  • Colours, fonts, layout and spacing
  • Where links and buttons point to
  • The intake form
About links: you can reword a button or link, but you cannot change where it goes. If a link needs to point somewhere different, that is a request for Charlotte, because a wrong address silently breaks the site for visitors.

Step Six · Everything Else

Visual changes go through BugHerd.

Anything beyond wording, a design tweak, a layout issue, something that looks wrong, a change to an assessment, a new link destination, goes through BugHerd exactly as it does now. Nothing about that has changed.

The BugHerd habit

1. Log in to BugHerd first at bugherd.com with your BugHerd account. If you skip this, the comment tool won't appear on the site.

2. Then browse the live site at executiveframe.co.nz — the BugHerd tool will be active in your browser.

3. Click the + pin, then click the exact element you want changed — the diagram, image, button or section itself.

4. Write a short comment describing what you'd like. A sentence is plenty: what it is now, and what you want instead.

5. BugHerd automatically captures the page address, the exact element, a screenshot and your browser details, and the request lands on Charlotte's task board — nothing else to send. She actions it and the site republishes.

Step Seven · Growing The Site

New pages start with a request.

When the site needs a whole new page rather than a change to an existing one, use the New Page Request form. It gathers everything Charlotte needs to build the page properly, in one pass, so there is no back-and-forth.

What the form asks for
  • The page title, and where it sits in the site's structure
  • Which arm of the practice it belongs to
  • A description of what the page should say and do
  • Any files you want included — documents, images, source material

Your submission is tracked automatically and Charlotte is notified the moment it arrives. The new page is then built, matched to the site's design, and published like any other change.
