From a coded sheet to a live display

The researcher codes in the Themescape spreadsheet, a structured sheet with a tab for data, a tab for the framework and a tab for participants. They export each tab as CSV and drop the files into the viewer, which checks the headers against a fixed contract, refuses anything that does not match, and renders once the required files are present. Data and framework are required; participants and settings are optional.

Three ways to look, two ways to work

A node network, an organisational hierarchy and a file tree. Work mode lets the researcher refine and reorder, with edits exporting cleanly back to the spreadsheet or to Word. Present mode is built for a talk, a supervision or a conference session. Search runs across codes and quotes, definitions sit beside quotes, and the palette is the researcher's own.

Verbatim is protected

Verbatim quotes are never altered. A separate refined column carries any presentation wording, and the viewer toggles between the two.

What it is not

It does not replace NVivo or ATLAS.ti. Those tools are for coding; Themescape is for seeing and sharing what has been coded.

NeuroTap

Origin: Launched September 2025, now moving under the company

What it is

A psychology informed system of tap to run automations. Small NFC dots placed at a desk, home, car or workplace are each programmed so that tapping a phone against one launches a personalised routine, such as sending a routine message, adding a calendar event or switching the phone into focus mode. The routines are built from an executive function assessment rather than chosen from a list.

Purpose in Integration Lab

It makes executive function support physical, carrying the work of a session into the places where routines actually break down.

Where it stands

The aspiration

NeuroTap becomes something a customer can set up themselves. In session delivery is capped by the hours Charlotte can spend on site, at 1 to 2 installations a week at most. In build

ClientBridge

Origin: Renamed from the Coaching with Charlotte app built for this practice

What it is

Structured support, between sessions. A practitioner facing platform that structures and sustains client progress between sessions. Practitioners assign tasks, set reminders and receive client progress reports, creating a continuous feedback loop that makes each session more effective. It is not a self help tool and it is not AI driven. It is a professional workflow platform that amplifies the practitioner's existing methodology, practitioner led and practitioner sold; clients interact with it because their practitioner has recommended it, not because they sought it out.

The problem it closes

The period between sessions is largely unstructured for clients and invisible to practitioners. Tasks discussed in session are forgotten, habits do not form, and progress stalls, so practitioners spend the opening of each session re establishing context rather than advancing treatment or coaching goals. ClientBridge makes between session activity visible, structured and accountable.

Purpose in Integration Lab

It is the stream with room to scale, sold by the practitioner seat rather than by the consulting hour, into a defined, regulated and reachable professional market.

Where it stands

In build, with a marketing launch target at the start of 2027. It evolved out of the Coaching with Charlotte application, a coach directed tool built for this practice, and is now modality agnostic. Development is led by a technical co founder who holds an interest in the product, so a registered entity, a shareholders' agreement and a formal assignment of the software are required before first paid launch.

Roadmap

Sequence as recorded in the business plan

Between sessions

A task carries text content, the task itself, a deadline, the next session date and reminder dates. The practitioner assigns it in session, the client acts on it in their own environment, and progress returns to the practitioner as usable information, so the next session opens with the practitioner already informed rather than reconstructing the fortnight from memory.

What it deliberately is not

Not a patient record system, not a habit tracker, not a self help tool, not telehealth, not an AI product. It sits between clinical practice management software and generic task apps, in a space neither serves well, as a structured accountability layer that belongs to the practitioner and is configured around their method. It contains no generative artificial intelligence, no automated treatment planning and no self directed client discovery, which reduces the compliance surface and keeps the practitioner, not an algorithm, in charge of the work.

What it produces

Faster client progress, reduced practitioner frustration, and a more defensible value proposition for the practitioner when discussing fees, outcomes or graduation timelines with clients.

Customisation over content

A basic set of starter tasks reduces setup, but the defining feature is that the practitioner tailors tasks to the individual client, keeping the work specific to the person rather than generic to the diagnosis.

Mobile, web or both: undecided

Practitioner subscription

A psychologist subscribes at 199 dollars a month, which covers up to 6 active clients on the platform. The practitioner is the buyer and the billable seat; clients are brought on by their practitioner and are not charged.

Why a flat monthly fee

The recorded tension is that a practitioner paid per session has no incentive to adopt a tool that shortens engagements. A subscription priced by practitioner and client capacity, rather than by session, keeps the platform's value independent of how many sessions a client needs, so a faster outcome costs the practitioner nothing.
